# Reading mode

Design doc: `docs/reading-mode.md` (§7 is what this slice deliberately leaves out).

- `src/lib/reading/` — **comprehension: a whole text, and what the app knows about every word in it.** Stateless on the same seam as `$lib/conversation`: **nothing here imports `$lib/db`**, the caller passes the vocabulary in and persists what comes out, which is what keeps the module testable in node and every write to the learner's collection going through the repositories that capture sync events. **Three envelopes, two doors and a lookup**, mock/real dispatch in `index.ts` on `isMockMode()` exactly as `getBatch` and `startConversation` make it, all pinned with `responseFormat` and re-parsed with zod (`schemas.ts`, mirroring `$lib/conversation/schemas.ts` down to the `strictJsonSchema` pass, `.nullish()` normalized to absent). **Generate** (`generate.ts`) writes a text *out of* the whole collection — vocabulary as terms only (no ids; capped by `MAX_VOCABULARY_TERMS` with a deterministic trim-then-slice), a `focus` list the text must use at least once, `SENTENCES_BY_LEVEL` for the length — and asks for `{text, reading, translation}` per sentence plus a glossary. **Annotate** (`annotate-call.ts`) is its inverse, for a text the learner brought: **the sentences are split locally first** (`sentences.ts`, pure and tested — sentence-final `.!?。！？…` plus every hard newline, closers riding along) and travel *to* the model numbered, so what lands on screen is exactly what was pasted, character for character. The alignment is the fragile part and is **all-or-nothing per chunk**: an array of the wrong length drops that chunk's readings and translations together and keeps its glossary, because a translation under the wrong line is worse than no translation. Named apart from `annotate.ts` on purpose — that one is free and runs on every open, this one is paid and runs once.

- **A long import is several calls, and `index.ts` is the orchestrator.** `MAX_IMPORT_CHARS` (4000) is now the budget for **one call**, not for the import; `chunkSentences` (`chunks.ts`, pure and tested) packs the sentences greedily into chunks of that size and never splits one, and `annotateReadingText` runs the chunks **sequentially** through the unchanged single-call `requestAnnotatedText`/`mockAnnotatedText` — sequential because a rate limit is the ordinary failure and a fan-out would either trip it or half-succeed, and because a failure should stop at the first chunk rather than after all of them. The merge is the whole of the new surface: sentences concatenated in order, glossaries concatenated and **deduped by `wordKey` (first wins)**, `usage` summed, and the **learner's `title` passed only to the first chunk** so the text is not named after its middle. `MAX_IMPORT_TOTAL_CHARS` (40 000) is the ceiling on one whole import and is enforced by the *page*, which is the layer that can refuse before the money is spent; `importCallCount(sentences)` is how it says "about N calls" first. `opts.onProgress(done, total)` fires once per chunk and only from this path.

- **Subtitles are an import format** (`subtitles.ts`, pure, dependency-free, no `$lib/db`). `detectSubtitleFormat` recognises SRT, VTT and a copied "Show transcript" panel and returns `undefined` for prose, so there is still **one door**: the learner pastes or uploads whatever they have and the page recognises it. `parseSubtitles` cleans a file down to `Cue { start, end, text }` — BOM and CRLF, `NOTE`/`STYLE`/`REGION`, cue identifiers, cue settings, every `<...>` tag including the per-word `<00:00:01.240>` timestamps, and the named entities — and **de-duplicates YouTube's rolling auto-captions**, where every cue repeats the line above it and a ten-millisecond transition cue sits between each pair: a line is emitted the first time it is seen, keeps the timing of that cue, and a cue left empty is dropped. `cuesToSentences` then **undoes the cueing**: the cue texts are joined with a space — or with *nothing* between two CJK characters, since a space there is a mark the source did not have — `splitSentences` cuts the join, and each sentence's offsets are recovered with a cursor and `indexOf` (the split is a cut, so every sentence is a contiguous substring of the join and they come back in order) and mapped to the cues holding its first and last character. The separator is deliberately **not a newline**, because `splitSentences` splits on those and would simply reinstate the cueing. A transcript with **no sentence-final mark anywhere** — the common auto-caption case — takes the per-cue path instead, since one giant sentence is unreadable and unpageable. The timings land on `ReadingSentence.start`/`end` (milliseconds into the media, both or neither) — zipped on **by the page**, by index, so the module never learns where the text came from — and what reads them is the reader's **follow view**, through `$lib/media` (`media.md`); nothing in `$lib/reading` does. Both return a `ReadingTextDraft`; **the page mints `id` and `createdAt`**. **Look up** (`lookup-call.ts`) is the third, the only paid call that runs *mid-read*: one word the glossary missed, sent with the whole sentence around it so a polysemous word comes back in the sense it is being used in, returning one `GlossEntry` whose `term` is taken from the **request** rather than the reply (the entry is about to be matched character for character, and a dictionary form would match nothing) — the page merges it into a `$state` `extraGlossary` it owns, so the word turns `new` everywhere for the rest of that open and is never persisted, and **only the button fires it, never a tap**. The two text prompts share their glossary rules verbatim (`GLOSSARY_RULES` in `generate.ts`): `term` is the form the text actually uses — never a base form, because matching is `wordKey` and nothing else — and a word is "in vocabulary" only when the *identical* term is listed, so a compound or an inflected form of one still gets its own entry.

- **Segmentation, and why the glossary is load-bearing.** ICU word boundaries are the base (`segmentWords` in `$lib/text`, `Intl.Segmenter`, no download) and **vocabulary, glossary and known terms override them by greedy longest match on top** (`termsFor`), so 自行车 stays one cell where ICU would cut 自行 + 车. The tokenizer is the romanizer's `tokenize` when the language has one (`$lib/romanize` — whole-sentence pinyin sliced per token, the only way polyphones come out right) and `tokenizeByTerms` otherwise; `annotate.ts` accepts either through `TokenizeFn` and never needs to know which it got. **Concatenating every token's `text` reproduces the input exactly** — a caller renders tokens instead of the string and must never have to diff the two. For a script written without spaces the glossary is therefore *also* a segmentation override, which is why both prompts say to be complete: a word left out of it falls back to ICU's cut — usually right, but 自行|车 shows what "usually" costs — and to a `plain` cell with no meaning behind it. `wordKey` — trimmed, NFC, lower-cased — is the one normalization, used on both sides of every lookup.

- **Four statuses, three acts** (`docs/reading-mode.md` §3 is the reasoning; LingQ is the reference point). A word is `tracked` (in the garden, with an FSRS card), `known` (understood, deliberately not scheduled), `new` (the text's glossary explains it) or `plain` (nobody has said anything about it — still tappable, with no gloss). The acts are kept distinct and must stay so: **tapping is a *lookup*** ("explain this"), which shows the gloss and records `wordLookedUp` and **does not add the word to the garden** — adding stays an explicit tap, because the garden is what gets scheduled and must not fill up by accident — and on a *tracked* word it is also a lapse, graded `Again` **once per word per day** through `updateItemAfterReview` (the drill's own call, so it is an ordinary ledger review — and per *day* is what unifies the two: a word lost in this morning's drill is not lost again by reading it); **the page's primary button** is the other half — every tracked word on that page read *without* a lookup **on that page** gets `Good`, unless it already has a `Good` today; the un-tapped `new` words are marked known **only behind a checkbox that is off by default**, never automatically (a tapped `new` word is never eligible, text-open-wide rather than per page) — and it confirms first because it writes a dozen events at once. **None of that state is held here**: it is derived from the item's own `recentGrades` (`getAllItems({ withRecentGrades: true })`, last entry, `localDay` equal to today), so it survives a reload and agrees with the drill; the only set the reader keeps is *what was tapped on this page*, cleared on every turn, because a word looked up on page one and read fine on page three is the re-encounter the mode exists for. **Every confirmation lives in the panel** — the slot the word card uses (facing page at ≥48rem, sheet at the foot of a phone), never in the header and never above the text — so the header keeps one shape whatever is being decided; **"I know this" is a status declaration** (`wordMarked`), not a review, and known words stop being highlighted or glossed *and are handed to the generator as vocabulary it may use freely* — that union is the whole point of the mark, so a caller building `vocabulary` sends items **plus** `getKnownTerms()`; **"Add to my words" is `add_words`, verbatim** from `$lib/assistant/tools`, which keeps it the one route by which vocabulary enters the collection and therefore the one place the term dedupe lives.

- **A text is immutable; everything adaptive is derived at render time.** `annotate.ts` is pure and deterministic in the house style — `now` passed in, the coin flip an injectable `rng`, no clock, no DB, no preference store — so a text written last month shows this month's knowledge. Reading visibility follows the learner's `RomanizationMode`: `'on'` keeps every reading, `'off'` none, `'adaptive'` hides a `known` word's, keeps a `new`/`plain` word's, and fades a `tracked` one against its strength. **That roll is memoised per word key in a `Map` the *caller* owns, created once per text open** — a challenge rolls once because it is one screen, but a word appears in several sentences of a text and one that showed its pinyin in sentence two and hid it in sentence five reads as a bug. Keeping the same map across a re-annotation is also what makes "the learner just added a word" cheap: only the new word rolls. `showSentenceReading` decides the stored sentence-wide reading (the fallback for every language with no local romanizer) by asking whether any word still *deserves* one, never whether any word kept one.

- `src/routes/read/` — **the pages own every write.** `+page.svelte` is the library and the composer: two doors as a segmented toggle, `MAX_TOPIC_CHARS` on the topic, and both paths ending in `addText` → `goto('/read/' + id)`. The bring door is **one door for prose and for subtitles**, in three parts — what the text is, what it will cost, what it plays alongside — and a single `$derived` `plan` behind all of them. **A brought file is an object, not a paste**: `bringFile` reads it and asks `detectSubtitleFormat` what it is, and anything recognised becomes `sourceFile` (`{ name, text }`) rendered as a card that states its own facts — format, cues, sentences, duration, calls — with a × that restores the box, while anything else *is* a paste and goes into `pasted` where it can still be edited. The **content decides, never the extension**, so a `.txt` holding an SRT is still subtitles and an `.srt` holding prose is still prose. The two shapes are alternatives and exactly one is on screen; `pasted` is kept underneath a card so removing the file gives back what was typed. `plan` derives from `sourceFile?.text ?? pasted` — still **one derivation over one source**, which is what keeps the card, the counter, the button's disabled state and `add()` from ever disagreeing — and carries `durationMs` (the furthest cue end) for the card. The cost row (sentences · calls, and `chars / MAX_IMPORT_TOTAL_CHARS`) sits **before the button whatever the source is**, and the two states that disable the button — over the cap, or a file with nothing readable in it — say so there, because a dead control cannot explain itself. `MAX_IMPORT_TOTAL_CHARS` is counted live against `plan.chars` — the *sentence* text for subtitles, not the raw file — and blocks the button rather than truncating the box (a `maxlength` would silently clip a subtitle file). Progress is `onProgress` into `annotateDone`/`annotateTotal` on the button's own label. `[id]/+page.svelte` is the reader: it builds the `AnnotateContext` once per load and **re-annotates after every write by reloading items and known terms**, keeping the roll map, so the text recolours in place under an open card that then shows the *new* status — which is why the open word is held as a position into the derived sentences and never captured on tap. **It reads a page at a time**: `paginate` (`pages.ts`, pure and tested, `PAGE_WORDS` = 30 packed greedily, a break only ever *between* sentences, always at least one) cuts the *stored sentences* — counted by ICU's base segmentation (`countWords`), never the annotated tokens, so a break cannot move when the learner adds a word — the current page is `?p=` (1-based, clamped, `page.url` from `$app/state`, written with `goto(url, { replaceState: true, noScroll: true })` and a scroll to the top), and nothing about the position is stored — the library always opens page 1. `lines` stays the annotation of the **whole** text because the roll map is text-wide; only `pageLines = lines.slice(start, end)` is rendered, and Listen, the translation and the stored reading follow it. Word positions in the markup stay whole-text indices (`pageRange.start + l`), so `selected` still points into `lines`. Words are real `<button>`s (Tab reaches them) wearing no button chrome, and the token loop is written **without whitespace between tokens** for the same reason `RubyText` is: the tokens reproduce the sentence character for character and a newline in the template becomes a rendered space between every pair of them. Layout per `layout.md` — the text column is `--measure` whatever the spread allows, and the word card is a facing page at ≥48rem and a sheet at the foot of the phone below it.

- **A text with a recording opens in the follow view, which is a *view* and not a route.** Everything under it is the same: the same `AnnotateContext`, the same `lines` over the whole text, the same whole-text `selected`, the same card. Only the page is cut differently — **following, the page *is* the current sentence**, `pageRange = { start: i, end: i + 1 }` for the sentence whose `start` most recently passed the clock (`sentenceAt` from `$lib/media`, and `{ start: 0, end: 0 }` before the first cue), so the translation, the stored reading, Listen and the whole-text word indices all follow the line being spoken without one of them knowing about a video. `followable` is **a `media` of either kind** and at least one timed sentence; which kind it is decides nothing beyond what the `.stage` mounts — a `<video>` for a file, an empty `<div>` for YouTube's iframe to fill — and both are wrapped as the same `Player`, so the effect, the transport, the auto-pause and the keyboard are written once (`media.md`). `?view=text` is the way to the paged reader and back, `?p=` dropped on the way, so the view is a URL fact like the page number and neither is stored; coming *back* to the follow view also clears a failed YouTube load, which is the retry.

- **The clock turns no grades.** Page grading is the learner saying "I have read this", and a video that keeps playing while they look out of the window says nothing at all — so the finish row, the `Good`s, the mark-known checkbox and the receipt are **rendered in the paged view only**. What a *tap* means is unchanged in both, because a tap is real: `recordLookup` still fires and a lookup on a garden word is still `Again`, once per day. A tap also **pauses** the recording, which is what the learner would do themselves one beat later, having lost a line. **Listen is not rendered while following** — the recording is the audio — and the header slot simply goes empty rather than reshaping.

- **The reader owns the player, and `$lib/media` owns everything else about it.** `bind:this` whichever element the stage holds, build the matching player in **one** effect — `videoPlayer` for the `<video>`, `youtubePlayer(frame, videoId, { onFail })` for the empty `<div>` — and tear it down in its cleanup; nothing below that line knows which it got. The object URL is its own effect whose cleanup is the revoke, so every replacement lets the previous file go; it and the picker and the session cache are **file-only paths** and a YouTube text touches none of them. `playable` is what the transport is disabled on — a file in hand, or an API that turned up — because "there is a player" is the same question with two answers. A YouTube load that fails puts one line in the picture's place and leaves the text and "Read as text" exactly as they were. `currentMs` is `$state` fed by `onTime`; the previous sample is a plain `let`, because it exists only to ask `crossedEnd` whether a boundary fell between two ticks and nothing renders it. Native `<video controls>` stays **on** — scrubbing, volume and fullscreen are free and better than anything written here — and our buttons add the operations a video does not have: replay the line (seek to its `start`, playing), next line (seek), play/pause, and an **auto-pause at line end that is off by default and never remembered**, since it turns a video into a drill. The keyboard mirrors them (Space, ←, →) and stands down whenever focus is in an `input`, `textarea`, `button`, `video`, `iframe` or anything contenteditable — and on a YouTube text it stops working altogether the moment the learner clicks into the player, which owns the keyboard from then on and is a caveat the transport states rather than one the page fights. **Position is not stored**: a text opens at 0, for the same reason it opens on page 1.

- **The transcript takes the panel's slot, and the card replaces it.** At ≥48rem the facing page shows every sentence as a list, the current one highlighted and `scrollIntoView({ block: 'nearest' })`'d as it changes, each line a seek; a word card or a confirmation *replaces* it, so there is still exactly one panel whatever is in it — which is why the transcript wears `.word-card`'s own skin (`--surface`, border, radius) and its sticky heading is painted `--surface` too: a heading in `--bg` reads as a bar laid *over* the panel instead of as the panel's top edge. It is **gated on a `matchMedia('(min-width: 48rem)')` `$state`, not on CSS**: hiding a thousand buttons with `display: none` still builds a thousand buttons, and a phone should build none. The phone base case is untouched — video sticky at the top of the column, the current line under it in the usual `.prose` markup, the previous and next sentences faint either side as plain un-tappable text (context and a seek, not reading), then the controls, then the word card as the sheet it always was. Two measurements there are the follow view's own, because its column is a picture and a handful of rows rather than a page: the current line keeps **two lines of room whether or not the caption wraps** (a one-to-two-line flip on every cue otherwise twitches everything under it, and the picture too inside the frame, since the stage is what gives), and the **half-screen the sheet has to be scrolled clear of is reserved only while a sheet is open** (`--tail`, a custom property so the wide frame's `padding-bottom: 0` still wins). The paged view keeps that tail permanently, because a page of prose already scrolls and adding the room on tap would make it scrollable in the same instant; the follow column is shorter than the phone, so a permanent tail is half a screen of nothing under the controls.

- **At ≥48rem the follow view is a frame, not a scroll.** The spread becomes `100dvh` minus the shell's `padding-block` — header row `auto`, content row `minmax(0, 1fr)`, `align-items: stretch` undoing `.spread`'s `start`, `min-height: 0` on both columns — and the transcript scrolls *inside itself* while the window does not scroll at all. The left column is a flex column in which the picture is the only thing that gives: the caption rows are `flex: 0 0 auto`, and `.stage` is `flex: 1 1 auto` with the video sized `height: 100%; width: auto` so the aspect ratio decides its width from the room left over. **The caption matches the picture**, not the column — the video's width cannot be derived from a stylesheet once its height is what sizes it, so it is measured with `bind:clientWidth` into `--film-width` on the column and the rows take `min(100%, max(var(--film-width), var(--measure)))`; the floor keeps a portrait or a small video from squeezing the line into a ribbon, and the `.pick` placeholder and the YouTube frame are measured the same way — one binding each, since a binding shared between two branches is zeroed by whichever unmounts last — so nothing jumps when the recording arrives. YouTube's frame is a bare `<div>` with no intrinsic ratio, so it declares `aspect-ratio: 16 / 9` and takes the same `height: 100%; width: auto` in the frame; its iframe is styled through `:global(iframe)`, because the API creates that element and Svelte's scoping class is never on it. The line goes up a notch at ≥72rem, where it is a caption under a large picture rather than a paragraph on a page. `has-error` names an extra row because the error banner is a `.spread-full` child that comes and goes and would otherwise land the columns in an implicit third row. At ≥72rem the cap comes off entirely — `.shell-follow` on `<main>`, a new class rather than a scoped `.shell` override — and the video column takes the width while the transcript stays `minmax(20rem, 28rem)`. **A recording is the one content width may enlarge; the prose beside it is still measured and centred.** `layout.md` carries the exception.

- **Media attaches at import and never afterwards**, because a text is immutable: the composer offers the recording **two ways and stores one** — a file input, or "or a YouTube link" beside it, **mutually exclusive**, each clearing the other as it is used (the file input is emptied by hand through a `bind:this`, since the browser owns its value and no state assignment reaches it). A link is parsed live by `videoIdFrom` and the hint under the field says which video it understood, or that it understood none, while there is still a box to correct; a file stores `{ kind: 'file', name, type }` and hands the `File` to `$lib/media`'s session cache before the `goto`, a link stores `{ kind: 'youtube', videoId }` and hands over nothing. Both are guarded on `timings`, because a recording a text has no offsets into is a reference nothing can follow — and that guard is **visible rather than silent**: the pair lives in a `<fieldset>` labelled "Recording · optional" that is **always rendered** and simply `disabled` (one attribute, no per-input bookkeeping) with a line saying subtitles are what a recording follows. A group that only appears once a paste happens to parse is a feature nobody knows the app has. On every later open a *file* text shows the picker in the video's place with the stored name, the line controls disabled until a file is chosen, and "Read as text" still working; a *YouTube* text needs nothing from the learner and only the network. `textAdded`'s zod payload has to name `start`, `end` and `media` explicitly — see `data.md`.
