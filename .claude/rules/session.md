# Sessions

Planning, generation and every play-time DB write.

- `src/lib/session/engine.ts` — the orchestrator: owns session planning (`planSession` — pure, tested), generation (`generateChallenges` → `getBatch` → `addToPool`), all DB writes during play (`applyResult`, `applyOverturn`, `reportChallenge`) and session accounting (`sessionSummary`). Components emit answer events; they don't write.

**A drill session is pure FSRS review of the vocabulary the learner already has, and generation never introduces a word.** The whole collection participates — a word added yesterday and never reviewed is due work like any other — so there is no mode, no filter and no `reviewOnly` option anywhere. New vocabulary arrives explicitly, through `add_words` in `$lib/assistant` and `$lib/conversation`. That is why `generateChallenges` makes no item write at all, why `getBatch` returns challenges only, and why the learn screen's answer to an empty collection is a link to `/converse` and `/chat` rather than a Generate button: with no words, there is nothing to write a lesson about.

## Sessions & challenge lifecycle

**Generation and play are decoupled.** Every challenge ever generated lives in a persistent *pool*, the `challenges` table (`ChallengeRow` = `Challenge` + `generatedAt`/`timesServed`/`lastServedAt`/`reported`/`topic?`), populated by `challengeAdded` events and materialised per `crates/sapling-core/src/materialize.rs`. Answering a challenge does not consume it — `applyResult` calls `recordServe`, which only stamps it. Serve-stamping at *answer* time is what makes an early quit self-cleaning: unplayed picks were never stamped, so they come back in the next plan for free.

`planSession(pool, items, now)` (pure, deterministic, tested) assembles a session behind three gates, two of them firm. *Playable* (not reported, all `itemIds` still resolve) is absolute, and so is a row's **kind eligibility**: `planBoard` drops any row whose kind is retired (`isActiveKind`, `$lib/llm`) or that a word it exercises has outgrown or not yet reached (`isKindAvailableAt(kind, difficultyLevelOf(item))`, both driven by `PLANNABLE_KINDS.levels`) before either planner ever sees it — a legacy wire shape or a rung-inappropriate row is not merely deprioritised, it does not exist for planning purposes. **Bearability** (below) is also firm now: a word gets served only what its weakest word can currently bear, never a fallback into an above-level challenge. *Rested* (never served or `RESERVE_GAP` = 3 days since) remains the one preference among the three — a due word may still get a shorter plan when every eligible row for it sits inside the reserve gap. Then **due-first** — each FSRS-due item, most overdue first, claims the best challenge covering it, and freshness only breaks ties and fills leftover slots. That ordering is load-bearing: a global freshness weight would let every new batch crowd out due reviews and quietly kill spaced repetition. The rest gap yields to it exactly once: a due word with no rested challenge takes its least-recently-served resting one in the first pass (otherwise a fast first two days serve-stamps the whole pool and leaves words due with nothing playable), while the second-angle pass and the freshness filler stay rested-only. **Then the same two passes run over the words that are *not* due yet** (soonest-due first, gap never spent in the walk), and the very last filler finally reaches into resting material — so a plan is empty only when the pool genuinely has nothing both playable and bearable for any word, and "Start session" is always live (a shorter-than-usual plan, rather than an empty one, is the ordinary result of a word whose only rows are above its rung). That tail is why there is no separate practice planner or second button: early review is native to FSRS (a review is graded whenever it happens, just a smaller stability gain), so a session simply degrades from due-work into review-ahead. What tells the learner to generate is **coverage** (`SessionPlan.topUp`, from `topUpCoverage` in `src/lib/session/topup.ts`): the same `planTopUp` walk generation makes, counted rather than planned. Its two numbers answer deliberately different questions. The **figure** is about the session in front of the learner — `covered / upcoming`, where `upcoming` is *every* due word when the schedule owes any and otherwise the next `SESSION_LENGTH` soonest-due ones, which is what a session would then serve; `due: boolean` says which, and changes nothing but the word on the label ("Due words with fresh challenges" / "Next words with fresh challenges"). The **count** (`wants`) is about the button, so it spans the whole walk and can be positive while every due word is covered — the press then buys the words further down the collection. The Generate button's label is "Write N new challenges" and the `notice` nudges when at most half of the figure's words are covered — all of them read the one walk, so the card can never nudge towards a button that would refuse. **At zero wants the button is disabled and reads "Everything's covered"**, which happens only once the learner's whole collection is covered. There is no "write more anyway" mode: `extra` existed only to keep the button live over a twelve-word window, and the window is gone. A topic colours the lesson and adds no wants of its own. A pool-wide count of rested rows stood here before, and it could say "running low" on a day every upcoming word was already covered (plan length means nothing either: the plan is never short). Difficulty rides *inside* that structure, in two layers: **bearability** (`$lib/session/progression`) — wherever the planner takes a candidate out of an item's rested or resting list it now takes "the nearest-fitting unpicked *bearable* one, else nothing", so a word gets recognition while it is new and production once its strength (`strengthOf`, derived by the core) clears the tier's floor (`CONSTRAINED_PRODUCTION_FLOOR` ≈ one successful review, `FREE_PRODUCTION_FLOOR` ≈ three). This is a serving rule now, not a preference: a word with nothing bearable in a bucket gets nothing from it, never an above-level fallback, and a word with nothing bearable anywhere among its eligible rows contributes no challenge at all — the plan comes out shorter, and coverage (below) is what tells the learner to generate, not an empty session. And then **fit** (`$lib/challenges/difficulty`'s `difficultyOf`), among just the bearable candidates: the one whose own difficulty sits nearest the **centre of that word's level band** (`levelBandCentre`, from `LEVEL_BANDS`) wins, so a strong word gets the harder of two challenges it can bear rather than merely the freshest one, with freshness/recency breaking a true tie. The band centre, not the word's raw strength, because the centre is the number `planTopUp` wrote the want at — and because a raw target degenerates at every band ceiling: a word in the upper half of any band is nearer its band's *hardest* row than its own strength, so the longest stale sentence would always beat the short fresh one written for that level. The preference is therefore two-sided only **within** the band. The two layers do not reach equally far: bearability filters any bucket the planner takes from, the fillers included, but **fit applies only inside one word's own bucket** (`firstFree`) — a `fitRank` is a distance to one word's target, so across words it is noise, and the heterogeneous fillers (`bearableFirst`) simply drop the unbearable rows, leaving `byFreshness`/`byRecency` order intact over what remains. Neither layer can cost a review or bend the rest gap. `$lib/session/progression` also has a five-rung `difficultyLevelOf` (levels 1..5 on the same two floors, plus `LEVEL_3_FLOOR` bisecting the tier-1 span and `LEVEL_5_FLOOR` well *above* both, inside tier 2) — `maturityOf` is just that ladder read at coarser resolution (1 → `'new'`, 2-3 → `'young'`, 4-5 → `'solid'`) — and `difficultyLevelOf` is the rung `planTopUp` writes every want at, so generation writes the kinds *and* the difficulty the planner would actually serve. `startSession()` reads pool + items and plans; session start never touches the network, so it is instant, always. The eligibility predicates themselves — `isPlayable`, `isRested`, `knownItemIds`, and `RESERVE_GAP` — live in `src/lib/session/pool.ts`, because the top-up planner asks exactly the same questions to decide what is *missing*.

**The free match rounds are spliced in at plan time, sized off the same ladder, and confined to early material.** `interleaveMatchRounds(challenges, items, rng)` restricts itself to the words at rung ≤ 2 (`difficultyLevelOf`) — matching pairs is a recognition warm-up, and once a word is mature the session should read as uninterrupted production, not free filler between hard sentences. It puts one locally built `match-pairs` round, drawn only from those early words, after every `MATCH_PAIRS_EVERY`th challenge that touches one of them (mature-only stretches of the plan pass by without a splice point at all), never after the very last challenge overall — a session must not end on free filler — so the whole session, paid and free, is one queue before anything walks it; the TTS warm loop is why, since a round improvised mid-play can never have its tile audio pre-rendered. Its **size** is `difficultyLevelOf` read over just those early words and reduced to their **median** rung, lower median on a tie: a round is one screen shared by several words, so one word further up the pair among new ones must not size it for all of them, and an average would drag it up by a fraction of a rung anyway. The rung is computed once from the frozen session vocabulary's early slice and handed to every splice point, so the function stays pure and deterministic given `rng` — and takes no clock, because a word's strength was derived when the items were read.

**The native-language line is shown or hidden at serve time too — `showNativeHint(challenge, items)` in `src/lib/session/hints.ts`.** A cloze's `translationHint`, a word-order's `prompt` and a spot-error's `meaning` are always written by generation (the model emits content, never presentation) and always on the row; the learn screen decides beside `planReadings`, once per served challenge, whether the components render the line, and hands the answer down as `showHint`. It shows while the challenge's weakest word (`weakestWordStrength`, the same one the readings ramp asks) is at rung ≤ 2 (`HINT_CEILING_LEVEL`, the floor the three kinds are first planned at) and is gone from rung 3, a step rather than a coin flip, so nothing is rolled or memoised. Serve time rather than generation time because a row outlives the rung it was written at: a hint baked in at rung 2 would still be on screen at rung 4, and a row written bare at rung 3 would have nothing to offer when a lapse drops the word back. Some rows written by an earlier build lack the line altogether; they render without it and stay playable.

**Word-bank size and distractor-tile count get the same treatment, in `$lib/session/support`.** `bankSizeFor(challenge, items)` (cloze, multi-cloze) and `distractorTilesFor(challenge, items)` (word-order) read `levelForStrength(weakestWordStrength(…))` exactly as `showNativeHint` does, off their own ladders (`CLOZE_BANK_LADDER` 3,3,4,5,6; `MULTI_CLOZE_BANK_LADDER` 5,6,7,8,9; `WORD_ORDER_DISTRACTOR_LADDER` 0,0,1,2,3 — the same numbers generation used to write with, now read at serve time instead), capped at whatever the stored row actually has. Generation always writes the fullest bank or tray its wire type allows (`llm.md`); which of those stored entries a served challenge actually shows is `visibleBank`/`visibleTiles`, deterministic from the row's own stored order (already shuffled once, at resolve time) — the answer(s) first, keeping their stored position, then the first surviving distractors in stored order, never reshuffled again. The learn screen computes `bankSize`/`distractorTiles` in `show()` beside `showHint`, and hands them to `ChallengeHost` through the same optional `ChallengeProps` fields the readings and the hint use — absent (a bare render, tests, `MatchPairs`) means "show everything stored". `Cloze.svelte`, `MultiCloze.svelte` and `WordOrder.svelte` render the `visible*` subset; grading still runs against `acceptedAnswers`/`answerTokens`, never bank or tile indices, so a trimmed bank or tray cannot break it.

**`planSession` closes with one small, local repair: `smoothDemand`.** Due-first order can still leave two adjacent challenges a full two demand tiers apart — straight from recognition into free production, no constrained-production challenge in between — because due-first, not difficulty, is what the walk optimises for. `smoothDemand` walks the finished plan once and, wherever it finds such a jump, pulls the *nearest later* challenge at the missing middle tier (`demandOf`, `$lib/challenges/demand`) forward to sit between the two; if the plan has no such challenge left anywhere after that point, the jump is simply left as it is. It is deliberately not a sort — due-first order is load-bearing, since an early quit must still have hit the most overdue words first — so this only ever swaps two challenges' positions, one pair at a time, and never re-ranks anything else. It runs inside `planSession`, so it sees the plan before `interleaveMatchRounds` splices the free rounds in (the learn screen calls that separately, after `startSession`), and a spliced-in round is therefore never itself mistaken for part of a demand jump.

Generating is an explicit user action (`generateChallenges`, no threshold on *when*) that the learn screen runs **as a background task** — `startTask('top-up', …)` in `$lib/tasks`, which owns the ledger, the cancel and the error; the page reads `taskStore.latestOf('top-up')` and re-plans in an `$effect` when one settles `done`, so a session can already be played from existing material; the new challenges simply land in the pool. The start screen shows only the button's running state and one line of outcome (the task's `summary`, or its `error` with a Try-again) — the step ledger lives in the task tray, not on the page. `generateChallenges` itself is unchanged and knows nothing about tasks. **What gets written is what the pool is missing, and nothing else.** `planTopUp(pool, items, now)` (`src/lib/session/topup.ts`, pure, injectable `rng`) is the whole brief: it walks the learner's **whole collection** — every word with a term and a meaning, in urgency order, due words most-overdue-first and then the not-yet-due ones soonest-first (`dueAt`/`isDue`, id breaking the tie so the walk does not depend on the store's read order), the same order `planSession` walks on the play side — and for each word it reads the word's rung (`difficultyLevelOf`) and the demand tier that rung can bear (`demandForLevel`, the same floors `bearable` gates serving on), counts the word's *coverage* (its rested, playable pool rows, by kind — `kindOf` from `$lib/llm`, the inverse of generating one), and emits a **want** `{item, kind, difficulty}` for each kind-group the word is short in: `WANT_PER_WORD` (2) — one recognition kind and one production kind when the word's rung has both sides available (`PLANNABLE_KINDS.levels`), otherwise two distinct kinds drawn from whichever side is available: two recognition kinds for a brand-new word with no production kind unlocked yet, and — at the top of the ladder, where recognition kinds have deliberately stopped being offered — two different production kinds instead of a lone one, for variety. A kind the word already has a rested row of is never asked for; among the rest a kind the word has *never* had in the pool wins, and `rng` breaks the tie; a word never gets the same kind twice in one top-up. **A covered word emits nothing and is simply stepped over**, and the list is capped at `MAX_TOPUP_WANTS` (24), cutting the least urgent words — which are still uncovered afterwards, so the next press starts with them. That is the whole of what one press versus the next means: one lesson writes for the words that can use it most, the one after it reaches further down. There is no window on the walk; a `selectSessionItems`-sized one used to sit here and it meant a collection with thirty due words got its third and fourth challenge about the same twelve while eighteen had none. A row the session would decline to serve — reported, orphaned, or above the word's bearable tier — is not coverage, which is why the predicates are shared through `./pool` (which also owns `SESSION_LENGTH`, since `./engine` imports `./topup` and both read it). Neither is a row of a retired kind or one the word's current rung has outgrown or not yet reached: `coverageOf` in `topup.ts` applies the same two firm kind filters `planBoard` does (`isActiveKind`, `isKindAvailableAt(kind, difficultyLevelOf(item))`, both from `$lib/llm`) before counting a row toward a word's coverage at all. **There is no accuracy dial and no recent-mistake list, on purpose**: FSRS already lowers a missed word's strength, which lowers its rung, which shortens every sentence written about it — one mechanism instead of two that had to be kept agreeing. So a press with the whole collection covered has nothing to ask for, and `generateChallenges` says so (a plain `Error`, rendered inline like an `LlmError`) before spending anything; `GenerateInfo.failedRequests` says how many requests came back empty, since their wants are simply still wanting. `planRefill(pool, items, profile, now, opts)` is the thin wrapper that puts those wants into `BatchArgs` beside the profile, `topic` and `knownItems`; the LLM layer plans nothing. `knownItems` (the whole vocabulary; only the terms reach the prompt, with a `romanization` riding along so `knownTermLabels` can tell two same-spelled cards apart) is what the model builds sentences *out of*, and a challenge may cite one of those words by term; `resolveBatch` drops any challenge whose references resolve to neither a known id nor a known term, which is the only enforcement needed, since nothing can mint an item. A topic still shapes what the review challenges are about. So **prompt/schema changes only reach the pool via newly generated batches** — old rows keep playing as they were generated, which is a recurring source of "bug" reports. The learner can flag a bad challenge from the feedback banner; `reported` rows are excluded from `getPool()` forever.
