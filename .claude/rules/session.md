# Practice

The practice stream, generation and every play-time DB write.

- `src/lib/session/engine.ts` — the orchestrator: it asks Rust for the decisions (`streamHead` and `nextPick` over it, `lowWaterMark`, `matchRound`, `isEarlyChallenge`, `planTopUp`, `topUpCoverage`, through `$lib/challenges/core`; they are `crates/sapling-challenges`' `stream.rs` and `topup.rs`) and owns everything around them — generation (`generateChallenges` → `getBatch` → `addToPool`), all DB writes during play (`applyResult`, `applyOverturn`, `reportChallenge`), the start screen's read (`practiceOverview`) and accounting (`sessionSummary`). `stream.ts` is the stream's memory between picks and its reads; `serving.ts` what a pick is made against. Components emit answer events; they don't write.

**Practice is pure FSRS review of the vocabulary the learner already has, and generation never introduces a word.** The whole collection participates — a word added yesterday and never reviewed is due work like any other — so there is no mode, no filter and no `reviewOnly` option anywhere. New vocabulary arrives explicitly, through `add_words` in `$lib/assistant` and `$lib/conversation`. That is why `generateChallenges` makes no item write at all, why `getBatch` returns challenges only, and why the learn screen's answer to an empty collection is a link to `/converse` and `/chat` rather than a Generate button: with no words, there is nothing to write a lesson about.

## Sessions & challenge lifecycle

**Generation and play are decoupled.** Every challenge ever generated lives in a persistent *pool*, the `challenges` table (`ChallengeRow` = `Challenge` + `generatedAt`/`timesServed`/`lastServedAt`/`reported`/`topic?` — generated from `sapling-challenges`' `PoolRow`), populated by `challengeAdded` events and materialised per `crates/sapling-db/src/materialize.rs`. Answering a challenge does not consume it — `applyResult` files it with one `recordAnswer`, whose serve stamp only stamps it. **An answer is one write**: the per-word reviews, the result row and the stamp are one `Backend` call and one transaction in the core (`data.md`), and what crosses is verdicts — the overall one and a passage's per-gap `itemVerdicts` — never grades, which the core maps; a crash can no longer file a review without its result. The one re-grade after it, `applyOverturn`, still files its `replaceLast` reviews one `reviewItem` at a time. Serve-stamping at *answer* time is what makes stopping self-cleaning: a challenge shown but never answered was never stamped, so it comes back in the next stream for free. **An overturned answer is undone exactly** (`applyOverturn`, after an escalation agreed a `wrong` answer should have counted): each wrong word's `Again` is superseded by a `Good` at the answer's own instant (`replaceLast`, so the card refolds as if it had been accepted), and `overturnResult` logs a `resultOverturned` naming the answer by challenge and `at`, which the difficulty replay reads as `correct` — `almost` where a gap of a passage was almost (`overturnedVerdict`) — so the ratings move as for a success (`data.md`). The result row keeps what was answered.

**Practice is one stream that runs until the learner stops** (`docs/challenge-difficulty.md` §10; `stream.rs`, deterministic under a seed and tested there). There is no plan: `src/routes/learn/session/+page.svelte` asks `$lib/session/stream`'s `PracticeStream.next()` for one challenge at a time, after the last answer's writes have landed, and the stream reads the pool, the words, the ratings and the profile fresh for each pick — so each pick sees the review, the ratings and the serve stamp the last answer wrote. **One list, one predicate.** `upcoming` (`stream.rs`) is the next words in urgency order — FSRS-due words first, most overdue first, then the words not yet due, soonest first, id breaking a tie; only words with a term and a meaning, since nothing can be written about the rest — each with how many rows are **available** to it. `available` is the one predicate, and the only one: *playable* (not reported, all `itemIds` still resolve — `pool.rs`' `is_playable`), an active kind, **not spent** (answered fewer than `MAX_SERVES` = 3 times — `pool.rs`' `is_spent`; a spent row stays in the pool, history and all, and its words simply want a new one), **not served this stream** (`served` — which also keeps a word FSRS re-queues within minutes after a miss from getting the same sentence back), *rested* (never served or `RESERVE_GAP` = 3 days since) **or merely resting when the word is due** (otherwise a fast first two days serve-stamps the whole pool and leaves words due with nothing), and nothing else — **fit never makes a row unavailable**. Among a word's available rows at every help level, the pick is the one whose *rolled* chance is nearest the target (below), a tie going to never served (newest first) before served (longest ago first). **The next pick** (`head`/`next_pick`, the engine's `streamHead(pool, items, now, { serving, ratings, seed, served, pass })` and `nextPick` over it) is the head of that list: it answers the head word and, unless it has nothing, a position into the pool with its help level (`{word, next?: {at, shown, chance, due}, instead?}`), so what plays is the row as stored, minus its bookkeeping (`challengeOf`), and a row this build cannot parse keeps its place and is simply never picked. **Serving waits on the head while a batch can help it**: while the most urgent word has nothing available, `next()` answers `blocked` and the screen waits for a refill to write for it — so the words the schedule owes are not passed over for words ahead with rows of their own just because a batch takes a while. **Only when no batch can help** does the page ask again with `next({ pass: true })`, and the head is passed for the first word further down *the same list* that has an available row (`instead` names it) — one list and one predicate still, the walk merely continuing past a word nothing can be written for now; the head stays first and is picked again the moment it has something. Due-first is load-bearing: a global freshness weight would let every new batch crowd out due reviews and quietly kill spaced repetition; and because the list carries on past the due words into review-ahead, there is no separate practice mode. **Stopping is always clean**: every answer is written as it happens, and a challenge never answered was never stamped, so the stop ✕ goes straight to the summary with no confirmation.

**Refill is the stream's to ask for, and it runs as a task.** It reads the same list: `plan_top_up` with a `limit` writes for exactly the words among the first `limit` of `upcoming` that are **short of stock** (fewer than `STOCK` = 2 available rows), and the stream's `limit` is the **low-water mark** (`low_water_mark`: the batch's time over the learner's pace plus two, within 4–20 — the stream keeps the median of its last ten answer times and times each batch it started from the task record, `startedAt` → `finishedAt`), so a batch comes back before the learner reaches the words it is for. After the first pick and after each answer lands, when the learner *can* write (a key and a connection) and no top-up is running, the page claims one with `PracticeStream.refill()` — `{scope: {served, asked, limit}, words}`, the scope a top-up should write for and the words it asks for, or `null` when none of those words wants anything — and starts `startTask('top-up', { profile, ...scope })` itself: `$lib/session` never imports `$lib/tasks`, the task input carries what the stream has shown so the planner judges rows exactly as serving does, and single flight is the runner's (`serial`). **A word is asked for once**: the stream keeps the words its refills asked for (`asked`, beside `served`, passed to the planner, where such a word wants nothing) until a row of theirs is served — so a word a batch dropped or left short ends at one request instead of re-firing after every answer, while a word that was served and later runs short may be asked for again. **A batch that failed or was cancelled wrote nothing**, so its words are given back: the page keeps, per task id it started, the words that claim asked for — never task state — and hands them to `PracticeStream.release` when the record settles `failed` or `cancelled`. **There is one claim at a time**: `refill()` on the page is a single in-flight promise every caller joins, and every pick awaits it first — so a quick Continue never reads `writing` before the claim made after the last answer has started its task, and never stops on a head that batch is about to write for. When the head is **blocked** (`next()` answers `{kind: 'blocked', asked}`): a batch on its way means **waiting** (a spinner and a Stop, the tray saying the rest; a batch the learn screen started wakes it too), then picking again when it settles; otherwise, if the head was not asked for yet, a batch for it is claimed and waited for; and when no batch can help — the head was already asked for, a batch failed or was cancelled, no key (mock mode), no connection — the head is **passed** (`next({ pass: true })`, above). A batch settling `done` while the stream waits picks again; one settling `failed` or `cancelled` passes the head straight away rather than asking again on the spot, so a batch that keeps failing cannot loop (its words, released, are asked for again after the next answer). Only when no word in the list has anything does the stream stop and say why — the failed batch's error, or no key, offline, everything asked for already — with a **Try again** beside Back to Practice, which starts a fresh `asked` (`resetAsked`). Because every row a batch writes is available to the words it was asked for, a batch written as asked always gives the head something. Mock mode never refills on its own: the doc's "no key" case, and the learn screen's Write-a-lesson button still writes a mock batch on request.

What the start screen shows is `practiceOverview()` — the due count, the first pick the stream would make, and **coverage** (`PracticeOverview.topUp`, `coverage` in `topup.rs`), read off the same list over the whole collection. A word is **covered** when it has a row available — the very predicate serving picks by; it **wants** when it is short of stock. Its two numbers answer deliberately different questions. The **figure** is about the session in front of the learner — `covered / upcoming`, where `upcoming` is *every* due word when the schedule owes any and otherwise the next `UPCOMING` (20) soonest-due ones; `due: boolean` says which, and changes nothing but the word on the label ("Due words with fresh challenges" / "Next words with fresh challenges"). The **count** (`wants`) is about the button, which plans with no `limit`, so it spans the whole list and can be positive while every due word is covered — a covered word with one row is still short of stock, and the press also buys the words further down the collection. The Generate button's label is "Write N new challenges", and the `notice` nudges (and the card is `urged`) **only in practice mode** — mock, no key, where the stream cannot write by itself — when the first word has nothing or at most half of the figure's words are covered; all of them read the one list, so the card can never nudge towards a button that would refuse. With a key and a connection the stream fills those gaps itself, so the card is framed as optional and never urged: what only a press does is write ahead before going offline, or carry a topic. Offline, neither can write, and a start screen with nothing to practise says so. **At zero wants the button is disabled and reads "Everything's covered"**, which happens only once the learner's whole collection is stocked. There is no "write more anyway" mode, and a topic colours the lesson and adds no wants of its own.

**Difficulty is ratings with margins, and fit only orders** (`docs/challenge-difficulty.md`; the model is `crates/sapling-challenges`' `ratings.rs`, its numbers derived data the core keeps, `data.md`). FSRS memory decides *which word* (the list's order); the ratings decide which of its rows and help levels it gets, and which kinds a refill writes. A right answer's chance is `sigmoid(word − challenge − slope × (length − centre))`: the word side `scale · ln(1 + stability) + base` (the card's stability, `ItemSrs.stability`, with two ratings every word shares — the words' average for a row about several), one challenge rating per kind and help level (`part_key`), and one length slope per kind against the middle of its range. **The pick rolls**: each challenge rating is drawn once within its margin (`Roll`) and the option whose rolled chance is nearest the target wins (profile `aim`: easier/normal/harder → 0.90/0.80/0.70, `data/model.json`'s `targets`), so a well-known rating is taken only where it fits and an unsure one now and then rolls into reach and is corrected by its answer — the only exploration. **The roll is seeded per stream position** (`PracticeStream.rollSeed`: the stream's seed, drawn once, plus the number served), so a peek, a pick after a wait and the pick after a refill agree until one is served. What a pick is made against besides the pool and the words is one `Serving` (`$lib/session/serving`) and the ratings (`getRatings`): the aim, the readings setting and whether listening is possible — those two bounding which help levels exist (`fits.rs`' `help_levels`), read from this device's preferences by `deviceServing` — and **`readings`, whether a row written now carries readings**: the target language is written in a script other than Latin (`$lib/romanize`'s `writtenWithReadings`), so the model writes a reading beside every target-language string. A stored row's levels are read off the row; `readings` is what a kind *not yet written* is judged at (`written_levels`), because the readings setting decides its levels — with readings off a Mandarin row is served only with its reading hidden. A language without readings is untouched by the setting. `practiceOverview()` and every pick read pool, items, the ratings and the profile; starting never touches the network, so it is instant, always. "Start practising" is live when a first pick exists, when the first word has nothing yet but a batch could be written (a key, a connection, and wants) — the stream then opens waiting on it — or when any word further down has something (`PracticeOverview.anything`, the passing pick), which the stream serves by passing a head no batch can help.

**The free match rounds are a pacing rule on the stream, confined to early material.** A challenge about a word whose strength bar is under `EARLY_STRENGTH` (0.3) counts (`is_early`; answered, and only a reviewing type); after every `MATCH_PAIRS_EVERY`th (4) such challenge the stream's next step is one locally built `match-pairs` round (`match_round` over `match_pairs.rs`: pairs drawn from the early words only, every tile label unique, five pairs, three at least), and only when a pick exists to follow it — the stream never ends on free filler. Matching pairs is a recognition warm-up; once a word is mature the stream reads as uninterrupted work. That is the one place strength steers practice, and it is pacing, not difficulty. A `seed` replays the rounds. Audio is warmed per challenge as it is shown (a round's tiles included), fire-and-forget, one clip at a time — sound never blocks play.

**Every serve-time presentation decision is the pick's help level, turned into a screen in one place, `serve.rs`, and handed down as one prop.** A cloze's `translationHint`, a word-order's `prompt` and a spot-error's `meaning` are always written by generation (the model emits content, never presentation) and always on the row; a cloze's/multi-cloze's word bank and a word-order's distractor tiles are likewise always written in full, and so are the readings. A help level is a view of that row (`challenges.md`): `presentationFor(challenge, shown)` (`$lib/challenges/serve`) answers the one `Presentation` — hint, bank size, distractor tiles, the reading shown or hidden as a whole, `listening`, and `shown` itself; the practice screen computes it once per shown challenge from the pick's `shown` (a match round is `plain`) and hands it to `ChallengeHost` through `ChallengeProps.presentation`, and every component resolves it with `resolvedPresentation` — absent (a bare render, tests) means "show everything stored, hint on, every reading on, not heard first", the one default that helper owns. Listening is decided here too, once, not inside `MultipleChoice.svelte`: a failed or silent clip still reveals the text there, so sound never blocks play. The same object travels further: `applyResult` records its `shown` on the answer (`ChallengeResult.shown`, what the difficulty model learns from), the learn screen captures it onto `Feedback.presentation` the instant a challenge is answered (so a later challenge swap can't change it), `FeedbackBanner` takes it as an optional prop, and `ask()` forwards it to `getEscalation` as `EscalationArgs.presentation` (`llm.md`) — a dispute is judged against what the learner's screen actually showed, not the full stored row. `visibleBank`/`visibleTiles` then pick *which* stored entries show at that size, deterministic from the row's own stored order (already shuffled once, at resolve time) — the answer(s) first, keeping their stored position, then the first surviving distractors in stored order, never reshuffled again; `Cloze.svelte`, `MultiCloze.svelte` and `WordOrder.svelte` render the `visible*` subset, and grading still runs against `acceptedAnswers`/`answerTokens`, never bank or tile indices, so a trimmed bank or tray cannot break it. Serve time rather than generation time because a row outlives how its word stood when it was written: one row serves a weak word at `pick-4` and, weeks later, the same word at `typed`. Some rows written by an earlier build lack the hint or the bank altogether; `steps_of` offers them only the steps they can show, and they stay playable.

**Local kinds are written without the model.** `generateChallenges` plans once to learn which words want anything, reads their cues (`cuesFor`, `data.md`), and plans again with the words that have one as `cued` — only those can be planned a local kind such as `cue-cloze`, ranked by the same roll as every other kind. `writeLocal` (`sapling-challenges`' `local.rs`) builds the local wants from the cues — the cue within the kind's lengths nearest the asked length — and returns them with the wants left for `getBatch`, a local want nothing could be built for swapped for the model-written kind the roll puts nearest, among those not asked for that word; `getBatch` is skipped when nothing is left. Both sets land in one `addToPool`, and `GenerateInfo.args` is what was sent to the model. A `seed` replays both plans and the local rows (`docs/video-clips.md` §6).

Generating runs **as a background task** — when the stream's words ahead want rows (above), or when the learner asks from the learn screen (`generateChallenges` either way, the learner's press with an optional topic) — `startTask('top-up', …)` in `$lib/tasks`, which owns the ledger, the cancel and the error; the learn screen reads `taskStore.latestOf('top-up')` and re-reads its overview in an `$effect` when one settles `done`, and the practice screen watches the records it started; practice can already run from existing material, and the new challenges simply land in the pool, where the next pick finds them. The start screen shows only the button's running state and one line of outcome (the task's `summary`, or its `error` with a Try-again) — the step ledger lives in the task tray, not on the page. `generateChallenges` itself knows nothing about tasks. **What gets written is what the list is missing, and nothing else.** `plan_top_up` (`topup.rs`; the engine's `planTopUp(pool, items, now, { serving, ratings, served, asked, limit, seed })`; in Rust a `Scope`) is the whole brief: it reads `upcoming` — the stream's list, with `served`, `asked` and `limit` for a stream's refill, or the learner's **whole collection** for a press — and a word **short of stock** (fewer than `STOCK` = 2 available rows) wants `STOCK` rows written, each a **want** `{item, kind, length}`. **The kinds are the pick run over kinds instead of rows**: one roll of the ratings per word, each active kind judged by its written help level nearest the target (`written_levels`: the steps it is written with, its reading as the setting and `readings` allow, listening where it can be heard) at the middle of its lengths, and the two nearest taken, each written at that middle length (`middle_length`: `short + (long − short) / 2`); a `seed` replays the roll, and a word never gets the same kind twice in one top-up. A row is judged at the length it was asked for, whatever length the model wrote. **A stocked word emits nothing and is simply stepped over**, and the list is capped at `MAX_TOPUP_WANTS` (24), cutting the least urgent words — which still want afterwards, so the next one starts with them. **There is no accuracy dial and no recent-mistake list, on purpose**: every answer already moves the ratings, which is what the next pick and the next kinds are read from — one mechanism, not two that had to be kept agreeing. So a press with the whole collection stocked has nothing to ask for, and `generateChallenges` says so (a plain `Error`, rendered inline like an `LlmError`) before spending anything; `GenerateInfo.failedRequests` says how many requests came back empty, since their wants are simply still wanting. `planRefill(pool, items, profile, now, opts)` is the thin wrapper that puts those wants into `BatchArgs` beside the profile, `topic` and `knownItems`; the LLM layer plans nothing. `knownItems` (the whole vocabulary; only the terms reach the prompt, with a `romanization` riding along so the prompt can tell two same-spelled cards apart) is what the model builds sentences *out of*, and a challenge may cite one of those words by term; the resolver drops any challenge whose references resolve to neither a known id nor a known term, which is the only enforcement needed, since nothing can mint an item. So **prompt/schema changes only reach the pool via newly generated batches** — old rows keep playing as they were generated, which is a recurring source of "bug" reports. The learner can flag a bad challenge from the feedback banner; `reported` rows are excluded from `getPool()` forever.

**The banner may offer the answered word heard in a video**: when a reviewing challenge is answered, the practice screen resolves its `itemIds` to terms through the words it holds and calls `clipsFor` fire-and-forget with them and the served sentence (the last of `Served.audio`: the challenge's target-language text, completed where a gapped one is spoken too); the core answers the best tier, deterministic (`data.md`), and the page picks one uniformly at random (`Math.random`), so a word is heard in different places. A result that lands after the next challenge is shown is dropped (`clipGeneration`), a failure or an empty tier is no clip, and `FeedbackBanner` renders `ClipStage` from it. Nothing is written and nothing waits on it.
