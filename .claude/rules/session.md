# Sessions

Planning, generation and every play-time DB write.

- `src/lib/session/engine.ts` — the orchestrator: owns session planning (`planSession` — pure, tested), generation (`generateChallenges` → `getBatch` → `addToPool`), all DB writes during play (`applyResult`, `applyOverturn`, `reportChallenge`) and session accounting (`sessionSummary`). Components emit answer events; they don't write.

**A drill session is pure FSRS review of the vocabulary the learner already has, and generation never introduces a word.** The whole collection participates — a word added yesterday and never reviewed is due work like any other — so there is no mode, no filter and no `reviewOnly` option anywhere. New vocabulary arrives explicitly, through `add_words` in `$lib/assistant` and `$lib/conversation`. That is why `generateChallenges` makes no item write at all, why `getBatch` returns challenges only, and why the learn screen's answer to an empty collection is a link to `/converse` and `/chat` rather than a Generate button: with no words, there is nothing to write a lesson about.

## Sessions & challenge lifecycle

**Generation and play are decoupled.** Every challenge ever generated lives in a persistent *pool*, the `challenges` table (`ChallengeRow` = `Challenge` + `generatedAt`/`timesServed`/`lastServedAt`/`reported`/`topic?`), populated by `challengeAdded` events and materialised per `src/lib/db/materialize.ts`. Answering a challenge does not consume it — `applyResult` calls `recordServe`, which only stamps it. Serve-stamping at *answer* time is what makes an early quit self-cleaning: unplayed picks were never stamped, so they come back in the next plan for free.

`planSession(pool, items, now)` (pure, deterministic, tested) assembles a session behind two gates, only one of them firm: *playable* (not reported, all `itemIds` still resolve) is absolute, *rested* (never served or `RESERVE_GAP` = 3 days since) is a preference. Then **due-first** — each FSRS-due item, most overdue first, claims the best challenge covering it, and freshness only breaks ties and fills leftover slots. That ordering is load-bearing: a global freshness weight would let every new batch crowd out due reviews and quietly kill spaced repetition. The rest gap yields to it exactly once: a due word with no rested challenge takes its least-recently-served resting one in the first pass (otherwise a fast first two days serve-stamps the whole pool and leaves words due with nothing playable), while the second-angle pass and the freshness filler stay rested-only. **Then the same two passes run over the words that are *not* due yet** (soonest-due first, gap never spent in the walk), and the very last filler finally reaches into resting material — so a plan is empty only when the pool genuinely has nothing playable, and "Start session" is always live. That tail is why there is no separate practice planner or second button: early review is native to FSRS (a review is graded whenever it happens, just a smaller stability gain), so a session simply degrades from due-work into review-ahead. What tells the learner to generate is `poolLow` (**fewer than `POOL_LOW_THRESHOLD` *rested* challenges** — plan length no longer means anything, since the plan is never short), surfaced as the start card's one `notice`. A third preference rides *inside* that structure, in two layers: **bearability** (`$lib/session/progression`) — wherever the planner takes "the first unpicked candidate" out of an item's rested or resting list it now takes "the nearest-fitting unpicked *bearable* one, else the first unpicked", so a word gets recognition while it is new and production once `wordStrength` clears the tier's floor (`CONSTRAINED_PRODUCTION_FLOOR` ≈ one successful review, `FREE_PRODUCTION_FLOOR` ≈ three) — and then **fit** (`$lib/challenges/difficulty`'s `difficultyOf`), among just the bearable candidates: the one whose own difficulty sits nearest the **centre of that word's level band** (`levelBandCentre`, from `LEVEL_BANDS`) wins, so a strong word gets the harder of two challenges it can bear rather than merely the freshest one, with freshness/recency breaking a true tie. The band centre, not the word's raw `wordStrength`, because the centre is the number `planTopUp` wrote the want at — and because a raw target degenerates at every band ceiling: a word in the upper half of any band is nearer its band's *hardest* row than its own strength, so the longest stale sentence would always beat the short fresh one written for that level. The preference is therefore two-sided only **within** the band. The two layers do not reach equally far: bearability reorders any bucket the planner takes from, the fillers included, but **fit applies only inside one word's own bucket** (`firstFree`) — a `fitRank` is a distance to one word's target, so across words it is noise, and the heterogeneous fillers (`bearableFirst`) are a plain stable partition that leaves `byFreshness`/`byRecency` intact. Neither layer can cost a review or bend the rest gap. `$lib/session/progression` also has a five-rung `difficultyLevelOf` (levels 1..5 on the same two floors, plus `LEVEL_3_FLOOR` bisecting the tier-1 span and `LEVEL_5_FLOOR` well *above* both, inside tier 2) — `maturityOf` is just that ladder read at coarser resolution (1 → `'new'`, 2-3 → `'young'`, 4-5 → `'solid'`) — and `difficultyLevelOf` is the rung `planTopUp` writes every want at, so generation writes the kinds *and* the difficulty the planner would actually serve. `startSession()` reads pool + items and plans; session start never touches the network, so it is instant, always. The eligibility predicates themselves — `isPlayable`, `isRested`, `knownItemIds`, and `RESERVE_GAP` — live in `src/lib/session/pool.ts`, because the top-up planner asks exactly the same questions to decide what is *missing*.

**The free match rounds are spliced in at plan time, and sized off the same ladder.** `interleaveMatchRounds(challenges, items, now, rng)` puts one locally built `match-pairs` round after every `MATCH_PAIRS_EVERY`th challenge and never after the last (a session must not end on free filler), so the whole session — paid and free — is one queue before anything walks it; the TTS warm loop is why, since a round improvised mid-play can never have its tile audio pre-rendered. Its **size** is `difficultyLevelOf` read over the words a round could be drawn from (a term and a meaning, the same filter the builder applies) and reduced to their **median** rung, lower median on a tie: a round is one screen shared by several words, so one mature word among new ones must not size it for all of them, and an average would drag it up by a fraction of a rung anyway. Hence the `now`. The rung is computed once from the frozen session vocabulary and handed to every splice point, so the function stays pure and deterministic given `rng` and `now`.

Generating is an explicit user action (`generateChallenges`, no threshold on *when*) that the learn screen runs **in the background** while a session can already be played from existing material; the new challenges simply land in the pool. **What gets written is what the pool is missing, and nothing else.** `planTopUp(pool, items, now)` (`src/lib/session/topup.ts`, pure, injectable `rng`) is the whole brief: for each upcoming word — `selectSessionItems`, due words most-overdue-first, topped up to `maxItems` with the soonest-due ones that are not due yet, the same degradation `planSession` performs on the play side — it reads the word's rung (`difficultyLevelOf`) and the demand tier that rung can bear (`demandForLevel`, the same floors `bearable` gates serving on), counts the word's *coverage* (its rested, playable pool rows, by kind — `kindOf` from `$lib/llm`, the inverse of generating one), and emits a **want** `{item, kind, difficulty}` for each kind-group the word is short in: `WANT_PER_WORD` (2) — a recognition kind and a production kind once the word can bear production, two recognition kinds before that. A kind the word already has a rested row of is never asked for; among the rest a kind the word has *never* had in the pool wins, and `rng` breaks the tie; a word never gets the same kind twice in one top-up; the list is capped at `MAX_TOPUP_WANTS` (24), cutting the least urgent words. A row the session would decline to serve — reported, orphaned, or above the word's bearable tier — is not coverage, which is why the predicates are shared through `./pool`. **There is no accuracy dial and no recent-mistake list, on purpose**: FSRS already lowers a missed word's strength, which lowers its rung, which shortens every sentence written about it — one mechanism instead of two that had to be kept agreeing. So a second press straight after the first has nothing to ask for, and `generateChallenges` says so (a plain `Error`, rendered inline like an `LlmError`) before spending anything; `GenerateInfo.failedRequests` says how many requests came back empty, since their wants are simply still wanting. `planRefill(pool, items, profile, now, opts)` is the thin wrapper that puts those wants into `BatchArgs` beside the profile, `topic` and `knownItems`; the LLM layer plans nothing. `knownItems` (the whole vocabulary; only the terms reach the prompt, with a `romanization` riding along so `knownTermLabels` can tell two same-spelled cards apart) is what the model builds sentences *out of*, and a challenge may cite one of those words by term; `resolveBatch` drops any challenge whose references resolve to neither a known id nor a known term, which is the only enforcement needed, since nothing can mint an item. A topic still shapes what the review challenges are about. So **prompt/schema changes only reach the pool via newly generated batches** — old rows keep playing as they were generated, which is a recurring source of "bug" reports. The learner can flag a bad challenge from the feedback banner; `reported` rows are excluded from `getPool()` forever.
