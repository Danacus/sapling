# Sessions

Planning, generation and every play-time DB write.

- `src/lib/session/engine.ts` — the orchestrator: it asks Rust for the decisions (`planSession`, `interleaveMatchRounds`, `planTopUp`, `topUpCoverage`, through `$lib/challenges/core`; the planners are `crates/sapling-challenges`' `session.rs` and `topup.rs`) and owns everything around them — generation (`generateChallenges` → `getBatch` → `addToPool`), all DB writes during play (`applyResult`, `amendResult`, `applyOverturn`, `reportChallenge`) and session accounting (`sessionSummary`). Components emit answer events; they don't write.

**A drill session is pure FSRS review of the vocabulary the learner already has, and generation never introduces a word.** The whole collection participates — a word added yesterday and never reviewed is due work like any other — so there is no mode, no filter and no `reviewOnly` option anywhere. New vocabulary arrives explicitly, through `add_words` in `$lib/assistant` and `$lib/conversation`. That is why `generateChallenges` makes no item write at all, why `getBatch` returns challenges only, and why the learn screen's answer to an empty collection is a link to `/converse` and `/chat` rather than a Generate button: with no words, there is nothing to write a lesson about.

## Sessions & challenge lifecycle

**Generation and play are decoupled.** Every challenge ever generated lives in a persistent *pool*, the `challenges` table (`ChallengeRow` = `Challenge` + `generatedAt`/`timesServed`/`lastServedAt`/`reported`/`topic?`/`correction?` — the last the difficulty model's learned correction for the row — generated from `sapling-challenges`' `PoolRow`), populated by `challengeAdded` events and materialised per `crates/sapling-db/src/materialize.rs`. Answering a challenge does not consume it — `applyResult` calls `recordServe`, which only stamps it. Serve-stamping at *answer* time is what makes an early quit self-cleaning: unplayed picks were never stamped, so they come back in the next plan for free.

**The session plan is `plan_session` (`session.rs`), deterministic and tested there.** The engine's `planSession(pool, items, now, { serving, target, limit })` hands it the pool as read and gets back **picks** — `{at, shown, chance}`: a position into the pool, the help level it is served at, and the predicted chance there — so what plays is each row as stored, minus its bookkeeping (`challengeOf`), at a help level decided once; a row this build cannot parse keeps its place and is simply never planned. It assembles a session behind three gates, two of them firm. *Playable* (not reported, all `itemIds` still resolve — `pool.rs`' `is_playable`) and an active kind (`WireType::is_active`: a retired wire shape does not exist for planning purposes) are absolute, and so is **fit** (`fits.rs`, below): a row is served only at a help level that puts its words inside the window around the aim, never as a fallback. *Rested* (never served or `RESERVE_GAP` = 3 days since) remains the one preference among the three — a due word may still get a shorter plan when every row that fits it sits inside the reserve gap. Then **due-first** — each FSRS-due item, most overdue first, claims the best challenge covering it, and freshness only breaks ties and fills leftover slots. That ordering is load-bearing: a global freshness weight would let every new batch crowd out due reviews and quietly kill spaced repetition. The rest gap yields to it exactly once: a due word with no rested row that fits takes its least-recently-served resting one in the first pass (otherwise a fast first two days serve-stamps the whole pool and leaves words due with nothing playable), while the second-angle pass and the freshness filler stay rested-only. **Then the same two passes run over the words that are *not* due yet** (soonest-due first, gap never spent in the walk), and the very last filler finally reaches into resting material — so a plan is empty only when the pool genuinely has nothing playable that fits any word, and "Start session" is always live when anything does. That tail is why there is no separate practice planner or second button: early review is native to FSRS (a review is graded whenever it happens, just a smaller stability gain), so a session simply degrades from due-work into review-ahead. The target is `BATCH_TARGET` (14) unless the caller names one, never above the ceiling (`SESSION_LENGTH`, 20, or the caller's `limit`).

What tells the learner to generate is **coverage** (`SessionPlan.topUp`, from `top_up_coverage` in `topup.rs`): the same walk generation makes, counted rather than planned. A word is **covered** when a rested, playable row fits it — the very check serving picks by, so a row counted as coverage is one a session would serve, and one it would never serve is never coverage. Its two numbers answer deliberately different questions. The **figure** is about the session in front of the learner — `covered / upcoming`, where `upcoming` is *every* due word when the schedule owes any and otherwise the next `SESSION_LENGTH` soonest-due ones, which is what a session would then serve; `due: boolean` says which, and changes nothing but the word on the label ("Due words with fresh challenges" / "Next words with fresh challenges"). The **count** (`wants`) is about the button, so it spans the whole walk and can be positive while every due word is covered — the press then buys the words further down the collection. The Generate button's label is "Write N new challenges" and the `notice` nudges when at most half of the figure's words are covered — all of them read the one walk, so the card can never nudge towards a button that would refuse. **At zero wants the button is disabled and reads "Everything's covered"**, which happens only once the learner's whole collection is covered. There is no "write more anyway" mode, and a topic colours the lesson and adds no wants of its own.

**Difficulty is one check, `fits`, on one learned scale** (`docs/challenge-difficulty.md`; the model is `crates/sapling-challenges`' `model.rs`, its numbers derived data the core keeps, `data.md`). A pick's predicted chance is `memory × sigmoid(skill − difficulty)`: memory is FSRS's retrievability of the words (multiplied), skill the words' learned skill (the lowest), difficulty the kind and help level's learned base plus the kind's slope times the row's length plus the row's own learned correction. It **fits** when the chance is no lower than the window's bottom and the chance given the words are remembered is no higher than its top; the window is the learner's aim (profile `aim`: easier/normal/harder → 88/80/70%) −15 to +12 points. Among one word's rows, the one whose best help level sits **closest to the aim** wins, freshness/recency breaking a true tie; the fillers take any row that fits, in freshness/recency order, because a closeness is to one word's aim and across words it is noise. What a pick is made against besides the pool and the words is one `Serving` (`$lib/session/serving`): the learned shared numbers (`getDifficultyParts`), the aim, the readings setting and whether listening is possible — the last two bounding which help levels exist (`fits.rs`' `help_levels`), read from this device's preferences by `deviceServing`. `startSession()` reads pool, items, the learned parts and the profile, and plans; session start never touches the network, so it is instant, always.

**The free match rounds are spliced in at plan time and confined to early material.** `interleave_match_rounds` (the engine's `interleaveMatchRounds(challenges, items, seed?)` sends each planned challenge's `itemIds` and gets back each slot as a planned position or a built round) restricts itself to the words whose strength bar is under `EARLY_STRENGTH` (0.3) — matching pairs is a recognition warm-up, and once a word is mature the session should read as uninterrupted work, not free filler between hard sentences. That is the one place strength steers a session, and it is pacing, not difficulty. It puts one locally built `match-pairs` round (`match_pairs.rs`: pairs drawn from those early words only, every tile label unique, five pairs, a smaller round down to three rather than none when words run short) after every `MATCH_PAIRS_EVERY`th (4) challenge that touches one of them, never after the very last challenge overall — a session must not end on free filler — so the whole session, paid and free, is one queue before anything walks it; the TTS warm loop is why, since a round improvised mid-play can never have its tile audio pre-rendered. A `seed` replays the rounds; without one they draw from the OS.

**Every serve-time presentation decision is the pick's help level, turned into a screen in one place, `serve.rs`, and handed down as one prop.** A cloze's `translationHint`, a word-order's `prompt` and a spot-error's `meaning` are always written by generation (the model emits content, never presentation) and always on the row; a cloze's/multi-cloze's word bank and a word-order's distractor tiles are likewise always written in full, and so are the readings. A help level is a view of that row (`challenges.md`): `presentationFor(challenge, shown)` (`$lib/challenges/serve`) answers the one `Presentation` — hint, bank size, distractor tiles, the reading shown or hidden as a whole, `listening`, and `shown` itself; the learn screen computes it once per served challenge from the pick's `shown` (a match round is `plain`) and hands it to `ChallengeHost` through `ChallengeProps.presentation`, and every component resolves it with `resolvedPresentation` — absent (a bare render, tests) means "show everything stored, hint on, every reading on, not heard first", the one default that helper owns. Listening is decided here too, once, not inside `MultipleChoice.svelte`: a failed or silent clip still reveals the text there, so sound never blocks play. The same object travels further: `applyResult` records its `shown` on the answer (`ChallengeResult.shown`, what the difficulty model learns from), the learn screen captures it onto `Feedback.presentation` the instant a challenge is answered (so a later challenge swap can't change it), `FeedbackBanner` takes it as an optional prop, and `ask()` forwards it to `getEscalation` as `EscalationArgs.presentation` (`llm.md`) — a dispute is judged against what the learner's screen actually showed, not the full stored row. `visibleBank`/`visibleTiles` then pick *which* stored entries show at that size, deterministic from the row's own stored order (already shuffled once, at resolve time) — the answer(s) first, keeping their stored position, then the first surviving distractors in stored order, never reshuffled again; `Cloze.svelte`, `MultiCloze.svelte` and `WordOrder.svelte` render the `visible*` subset, and grading still runs against `acceptedAnswers`/`answerTokens`, never bank or tile indices, so a trimmed bank or tray cannot break it. Serve time rather than generation time because a row outlives how its word stood when it was written: one row serves a weak word at `pick-4` and, weeks later, the same word at `typed`. Some rows written by an earlier build lack the hint or the bank altogether; `steps_of` offers them only the steps they can show, and they stay playable.

Generating is an explicit user action (`generateChallenges`, no threshold on *when*) that the learn screen runs **as a background task** — `startTask('top-up', …)` in `$lib/tasks`, which owns the ledger, the cancel and the error; the page reads `taskStore.latestOf('top-up')` and re-plans in an `$effect` when one settles `done`, so a session can already be played from existing material; the new challenges simply land in the pool. The start screen shows only the button's running state and one line of outcome (the task's `summary`, or its `error` with a Try-again) — the step ledger lives in the task tray, not on the page. `generateChallenges` itself knows nothing about tasks. **What gets written is what the pool is missing, and nothing else.** `plan_top_up` (`topup.rs`; the engine's `planTopUp(pool, items, now, { serving, seed })`) is the whole brief: it walks the learner's **whole collection** — every word with a term and a meaning, in urgency order, due words most-overdue-first and then the not-yet-due ones soonest-first, id breaking the tie so the walk does not depend on the store's read order, the same order `plan_session` walks on the play side — and a word no rested, playable row **fits** (the same check serving picks by) wants `WANT_PER_WORD` (2) rows written, each a **want** `{item, kind, length}`. The kinds are those that can **reach** the word: some help level of a freshly written row of the kind, at some length in the kind's range (`data/kinds.json`), puts the word inside the window, planning at the memory a due word is served at (`WRITE_MEMORY`, 0.9, FSRS's target retention). Among them a kind the word has *never* had in the pool wins, then the one it has fewest fitting rows of, and a draw breaks the tie (a `seed` replays it); a word never gets the same kind twice in one top-up. The **length** is worked back from the difficulty that would put the word at the aim, at the midpoint of the kind's easiest and hardest help level, and clamped to its range (`length_for`): written in the middle, the row still fits when the word weakens (an easier help level) or strengthens (a harder one), so one row covers a wider range of skill. **A covered word emits nothing and is simply stepped over**, and the list is capped at `MAX_TOPUP_WANTS` (24), cutting the least urgent words — which are still uncovered afterwards, so the next press starts with them. There is no window on the walk: a twelve-word one used to sit here and it meant a collection with thirty due words got its third and fourth challenge about the same twelve while eighteen had none. **There is no accuracy dial and no recent-mistake list, on purpose**: every answer already moves the word's skill and the row's correction, which is what the next pick and the next length are read from — one mechanism, not two that had to be kept agreeing. So a press with the whole collection covered has nothing to ask for, and `generateChallenges` says so (a plain `Error`, rendered inline like an `LlmError`) before spending anything; `GenerateInfo.failedRequests` says how many requests came back empty, since their wants are simply still wanting. `planRefill(pool, items, profile, now, opts)` is the thin wrapper that puts those wants into `BatchArgs` beside the profile, `topic` and `knownItems`; the LLM layer plans nothing. `knownItems` (the whole vocabulary; only the terms reach the prompt, with a `romanization` riding along so the prompt can tell two same-spelled cards apart) is what the model builds sentences *out of*, and a challenge may cite one of those words by term; the resolver drops any challenge whose references resolve to neither a known id nor a known term, which is the only enforcement needed, since nothing can mint an item. So **prompt/schema changes only reach the pool via newly generated batches** — old rows keep playing as they were generated, which is a recurring source of "bug" reports. The learner can flag a bad challenge from the feedback banner; `reported` rows are excluded from `getPool()` forever.
