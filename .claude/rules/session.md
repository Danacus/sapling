# Practice

The practice stream, generation and every play-time DB write.

- `src/lib/session/engine.ts` — the orchestrator: it asks Rust for the decisions (`nextPick`, `streamOutlook`, `lowWaterMark`, `matchRound`, `isEarlyChallenge`, `planTopUp`, `topUpCoverage`, through `$lib/challenges/core`; they are `crates/sapling-challenges`' `stream.rs` and `topup.rs`) and owns everything around them — generation (`generateChallenges` → `getBatch` → `addToPool`), all DB writes during play (`applyResult`, `amendResult`, `applyOverturn`, `reportChallenge`), the start screen's read (`practiceOverview`) and accounting (`sessionSummary`). `stream.ts` is the stream's memory between picks and its reads; `serving.ts` what a pick is made against. Components emit answer events; they don't write.

**Practice is pure FSRS review of the vocabulary the learner already has, and generation never introduces a word.** The whole collection participates — a word added yesterday and never reviewed is due work like any other — so there is no mode, no filter and no `reviewOnly` option anywhere. New vocabulary arrives explicitly, through `add_words` in `$lib/assistant` and `$lib/conversation`. That is why `generateChallenges` makes no item write at all, why `getBatch` returns challenges only, and why the learn screen's answer to an empty collection is a link to `/converse` and `/chat` rather than a Generate button: with no words, there is nothing to write a lesson about.

## Sessions & challenge lifecycle

**Generation and play are decoupled.** Every challenge ever generated lives in a persistent *pool*, the `challenges` table (`ChallengeRow` = `Challenge` + `generatedAt`/`timesServed`/`lastServedAt`/`reported`/`topic?`/`correction?` — the last the difficulty model's learned correction for the row — generated from `sapling-challenges`' `PoolRow`), populated by `challengeAdded` events and materialised per `crates/sapling-db/src/materialize.rs`. Answering a challenge does not consume it — `applyResult` calls `recordServe`, which only stamps it. Serve-stamping at *answer* time is what makes stopping self-cleaning: a challenge shown but never answered was never stamped, so it comes back in the next stream for free.

**Practice is one stream that runs until the learner stops** (`docs/challenge-difficulty.md` §11; `stream.rs`, deterministic and tested there). There is no plan: `src/routes/learn/session/+page.svelte` asks `$lib/session/stream`'s `PracticeStream.next()` for one challenge at a time, after the last answer's writes have landed, and the stream reads the pool, the words, the learned parts and the profile fresh for each pick — so each pick sees the review, the skill and the serve stamp the last answer wrote. **The next pick** (`next_pick`, the engine's `nextPick(pool, items, now, { serving, served })`) is the most urgent word with a row that fits it: FSRS-due words first, most overdue first, then the words not yet due, soonest first, id breaking a tie; among that word's rested rows the one whose best help level sits closest to the aim wins, freshness breaking a true tie. It answers a position into the pool with its help level (`{at, shown, chance, due}`), so what plays is the row as stored, minus its bookkeeping (`challengeOf`), and a row this build cannot parse keeps its place and is simply never picked. Three gates, two of them firm: *playable* (not reported, all `itemIds` still resolve — `pool.rs`' `is_playable`) and an active kind are absolute, and so is **fit** (below) — never a fallback into a row that does not fit. *Rested* (never served or `RESERVE_GAP` = 3 days since) is the preference: a due word with nothing rested takes its least-recently-served resting row (otherwise a fast first two days serve-stamps the whole pool and leaves words due with nothing playable), a word not yet due waits the gap out, and **no row is shown twice in one stream** (`served`), which is also what keeps a word FSRS re-queues within minutes after a miss from getting the same sentence back. Due-first is load-bearing: a global freshness weight would let every new batch crowd out due reviews and quietly kill spaced repetition; and because the walk carries on past the due words into review-ahead, there is no separate practice mode. **Stopping is always clean**: every answer is written as it happens, and a challenge never answered was never stamped, so the stop ✕ goes straight to the summary with no confirmation. The stream ends on its own only when nothing fits any word and no batch can help (below), and says why.

**Refill is the stream's to ask for, and it runs as a task.** After each answer lands, the page asks the stream's `outlook()` (`outlook` in `stream.rs`: of the next `UPCOMING` (20) words in urgency order, how many have a pick ready; how many are due; what a top-up would write) and `shouldRefill` decides: the ready words are under the **low-water mark**, a top-up has something to write, one is not already running, and the learner *can* write — a key and a connection. The mark (`low_water_mark`) is the batch's time over the learner's pace plus two, within 4–20: the stream keeps the median of its last ten answer times and times each batch it started from the task record (`startedAt` → `finishedAt`), so a batch comes back before the ready words run out. The page starts `startTask('top-up', { profile })` itself — `$lib/session` never imports `$lib/tasks` — and keeps only the ids it started, never task state. When nothing fits: a batch on its way means **waiting** (a spinner and a Stop, the tray saying the rest), then picking again when it settles; a batch that can be started is started and waited for; a batch that came back and still left nothing that fits, a failed or cancelled one, no key (mock mode), no connection, or nothing left to write ends the stream with that reason. Mock mode never refills on its own: the doc's "no key" case, and the learn screen's Write-a-lesson button still writes a mock batch on request.

What the start screen shows is `practiceOverview()` — the due count, the first pick the stream would make, and **coverage** (`PracticeOverview.topUp`, from `top_up_coverage` in `topup.rs`): the same walk generation makes, counted rather than planned. A word is **covered** when a rested, playable row fits it — the very check serving picks by, so a row counted as coverage is one a session would serve, and one it would never serve is never coverage. Its two numbers answer deliberately different questions. The **figure** is about the session in front of the learner — `covered / upcoming`, where `upcoming` is *every* due word when the schedule owes any and otherwise the next `UPCOMING` soonest-due ones, which is what the stream would then serve; `due: boolean` says which, and changes nothing but the word on the label ("Due words with fresh challenges" / "Next words with fresh challenges"). The **count** (`wants`) is about the button, so it spans the whole walk and can be positive while every due word is covered — the press then buys the words further down the collection. The Generate button's label is "Write N new challenges" and the `notice` nudges when at most half of the figure's words are covered — all of them read the one walk, so the card can never nudge towards a button that would refuse. **At zero wants the button is disabled and reads "Everything's covered"**, which happens only once the learner's whole collection is covered. There is no "write more anyway" mode, and a topic colours the lesson and adds no wants of its own.

**Difficulty is one check, `fits`, on one learned scale** (`docs/challenge-difficulty.md`; the model is `crates/sapling-challenges`' `model.rs`, its numbers derived data the core keeps, `data.md`). A pick's predicted chance is `memory × sigmoid(skill − difficulty)`: memory is FSRS's retrievability of the words (multiplied), skill the words' learned skill (the lowest), difficulty the kind and help level's learned base plus the kind's slope times the row's length plus the row's own learned correction. It **fits** when the chance is no lower than the window's bottom and the chance given the words are remembered is no higher than its top; the window is the learner's aim (profile `aim`: easier/normal/harder → 88/80/70%) −15 to +12 points. Among one word's rows, the one whose best help level sits **closest to the aim** wins, freshness/recency breaking a true tie; the fillers take any row that fits, in freshness/recency order, because a closeness is to one word's aim and across words it is noise. What a pick is made against besides the pool and the words is one `Serving` (`$lib/session/serving`): the learned shared numbers (`getDifficultyParts`), the aim, the readings setting and whether listening is possible — the last two bounding which help levels exist (`fits.rs`' `help_levels`), read from this device's preferences by `deviceServing`. `practiceOverview()` and every pick read pool, items, the learned parts and the profile; starting never touches the network, so it is instant, always. "Start practising" is live when a first pick exists, or when nothing fits yet but a batch could be written (a key, a connection, and wants) — the stream then opens waiting on it.

**The free match rounds are a pacing rule on the stream, confined to early material.** A challenge about a word whose strength bar is under `EARLY_STRENGTH` (0.3) counts (`is_early`; answered, and only a reviewing type); after every `MATCH_PAIRS_EVERY`th (4) such challenge the stream's next step is one locally built `match-pairs` round (`match_round` over `match_pairs.rs`: pairs drawn from the early words only, every tile label unique, five pairs, three at least), and only when a pick exists to follow it — the stream never ends on free filler. Matching pairs is a recognition warm-up; once a word is mature the stream reads as uninterrupted work. That is the one place strength steers practice, and it is pacing, not difficulty. A `seed` replays the rounds. Audio is warmed per challenge as it is shown (a round's tiles included), fire-and-forget, one clip at a time — sound never blocks play.

**Every serve-time presentation decision is the pick's help level, turned into a screen in one place, `serve.rs`, and handed down as one prop.** A cloze's `translationHint`, a word-order's `prompt` and a spot-error's `meaning` are always written by generation (the model emits content, never presentation) and always on the row; a cloze's/multi-cloze's word bank and a word-order's distractor tiles are likewise always written in full, and so are the readings. A help level is a view of that row (`challenges.md`): `presentationFor(challenge, shown)` (`$lib/challenges/serve`) answers the one `Presentation` — hint, bank size, distractor tiles, the reading shown or hidden as a whole, `listening`, and `shown` itself; the practice screen computes it once per shown challenge from the pick's `shown` (a match round is `plain`) and hands it to `ChallengeHost` through `ChallengeProps.presentation`, and every component resolves it with `resolvedPresentation` — absent (a bare render, tests) means "show everything stored, hint on, every reading on, not heard first", the one default that helper owns. Listening is decided here too, once, not inside `MultipleChoice.svelte`: a failed or silent clip still reveals the text there, so sound never blocks play. The same object travels further: `applyResult` records its `shown` on the answer (`ChallengeResult.shown`, what the difficulty model learns from), the learn screen captures it onto `Feedback.presentation` the instant a challenge is answered (so a later challenge swap can't change it), `FeedbackBanner` takes it as an optional prop, and `ask()` forwards it to `getEscalation` as `EscalationArgs.presentation` (`llm.md`) — a dispute is judged against what the learner's screen actually showed, not the full stored row. `visibleBank`/`visibleTiles` then pick *which* stored entries show at that size, deterministic from the row's own stored order (already shuffled once, at resolve time) — the answer(s) first, keeping their stored position, then the first surviving distractors in stored order, never reshuffled again; `Cloze.svelte`, `MultiCloze.svelte` and `WordOrder.svelte` render the `visible*` subset, and grading still runs against `acceptedAnswers`/`answerTokens`, never bank or tile indices, so a trimmed bank or tray cannot break it. Serve time rather than generation time because a row outlives how its word stood when it was written: one row serves a weak word at `pick-4` and, weeks later, the same word at `typed`. Some rows written by an earlier build lack the hint or the bank altogether; `steps_of` offers them only the steps they can show, and they stay playable.

Generating runs **as a background task** — when the stream runs low (above), or when the learner asks from the learn screen (`generateChallenges` either way, the learner's press with an optional topic) — `startTask('top-up', …)` in `$lib/tasks`, which owns the ledger, the cancel and the error; the learn screen reads `taskStore.latestOf('top-up')` and re-reads its overview in an `$effect` when one settles `done`, and the practice screen watches the records it started; practice can already run from existing material, and the new challenges simply land in the pool, where the next pick finds them. The start screen shows only the button's running state and one line of outcome (the task's `summary`, or its `error` with a Try-again) — the step ledger lives in the task tray, not on the page. `generateChallenges` itself knows nothing about tasks. **What gets written is what the pool is missing, and nothing else.** `plan_top_up` (`topup.rs`; the engine's `planTopUp(pool, items, now, { serving, seed })`) is the whole brief: it walks the learner's **whole collection** — every word with a term and a meaning, in urgency order, due words most-overdue-first and then the not-yet-due ones soonest-first, id breaking the tie so the walk does not depend on the store's read order, the same order `plan_session` walks on the play side — and a word no rested, playable row **fits** (the same check serving picks by) wants `WANT_PER_WORD` (2) rows written, each a **want** `{item, kind, length}`. The kinds are those that can **reach** the word: some help level of a freshly written row of the kind, at some length in the kind's range (`data/kinds.json`), puts the word inside the window, planning at the memory a due word is served at (`WRITE_MEMORY`, 0.9, FSRS's target retention). Among them a kind the word has *never* had in the pool wins, then the one it has fewest fitting rows of, and a draw breaks the tie (a `seed` replays it); a word never gets the same kind twice in one top-up. The **length** is worked back from the difficulty that would put the word at the aim, at the midpoint of the kind's easiest and hardest help level, and clamped to its range (`length_for`): written in the middle, the row still fits when the word weakens (an easier help level) or strengthens (a harder one), so one row covers a wider range of skill. **A covered word emits nothing and is simply stepped over**, and the list is capped at `MAX_TOPUP_WANTS` (24), cutting the least urgent words — which are still uncovered afterwards, so the next press starts with them. There is no window on the walk: a twelve-word one used to sit here and it meant a collection with thirty due words got its third and fourth challenge about the same twelve while eighteen had none. **There is no accuracy dial and no recent-mistake list, on purpose**: every answer already moves the word's skill and the row's correction, which is what the next pick and the next length are read from — one mechanism, not two that had to be kept agreeing. So a press with the whole collection covered has nothing to ask for, and `generateChallenges` says so (a plain `Error`, rendered inline like an `LlmError`) before spending anything; `GenerateInfo.failedRequests` says how many requests came back empty, since their wants are simply still wanting. `planRefill(pool, items, profile, now, opts)` is the thin wrapper that puts those wants into `BatchArgs` beside the profile, `topic` and `knownItems`; the LLM layer plans nothing. `knownItems` (the whole vocabulary; only the terms reach the prompt, with a `romanization` riding along so the prompt can tell two same-spelled cards apart) is what the model builds sentences *out of*, and a challenge may cite one of those words by term; the resolver drops any challenge whose references resolve to neither a known id nor a known term, which is the only enforcement needed, since nothing can mint an item. So **prompt/schema changes only reach the pool via newly generated batches** — old rows keep playing as they were generated, which is a recurring source of "bug" reports. The learner can flag a bad challenge from the feedback banner; `reported` rows are excluded from `getPool()` forever.
